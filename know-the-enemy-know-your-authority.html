Know the Enemy, Know Your Authority

Strategy, Discernment & Victory in Christ

Central account: Acts 16:16–34 | Anchor: Ephesians 6:11–13 | Supporting: 2 Corinthians 2:11; Luke 10:17–20; Hebrews 5:11–14

1 | Historical context: God directs the mission (Acts 16:6–15)

During Paul’s second missionary journey, the Holy Spirit prevented him from entering Asia and Bithynia (Acts 16:6–7). A vision then directed him toward Macedonia (v. 9). Closed doors were not necessarily opposition; God was guiding the mission.

In Philippi, Paul met women gathered for prayer, including Lydia, whose heart the Lord opened to receive the message (v. 14). Later, while going to prayer, Paul encountered a slave girl with a spirit of divination. He had not sought a confrontation; it arose as he followed God’s direction.

Discernment begins with obedience, not a search for spiritual battles. Not every obstacle is an attack; sometimes God redirects us, and sometimes faithfulness leads through difficulty.

2 | Recognizing deception (Acts 16:16–18; 2 Corinthians 2:11)

Acts 16:16–18. 16 Once when we were going to the place of prayer, we were met by a female slave who had a spirit by which she predicted the future. She earned a great deal of money for her owners by fortune-telling. 17 She followed Paul and the rest of us, shouting, “These men are servants of the Most High God, who are telling you the way to be saved.” 18 She kept this up for many days. Finally Paul became so annoyed that he turned around and said to the spirit, “In the name of Jesus Christ I command you to come out of her!” At that moment the spirit left her.

Verse 16 — The encounter: The girl had a spirit of divination, and her owners profited from her fortune-telling. Her bondage was spiritual, social, and economic: the passage exposes both deception and exploitation.

Verse 17 — A truthful-sounding message: She called Paul’s group servants of the Most High God who proclaimed salvation. Yet Luke identifies her divination as the source of her activity. Words that sound true do not, by themselves, establish a trustworthy spiritual source.

Verse 18 — Authority in Jesus’ name: After many days, Paul commanded the spirit to leave in Jesus Christ’s name, and it departed. Luke does not explain all of Paul’s motives or why he waited. The power belonged to Christ, not Paul.

Question: “If the slave girl was speaking the truth about Paul and his companions, why do you think Paul eventually commanded the spirit to leave her?”

Recognizing the Enemy’s Strategies 2 Corinthians 2:11

The girl’s words sounded truthful, but their source mattered. Discernment therefore examines more than a message’s appearance; it asks whether its source and teaching align with God’s truth.

In 2 Corinthians 2:11, Paul writes: “In order that Satan might not outwit us. For we are not unaware of his schemes.”

The Greek noēma (νόημα), translated “schemes,” means a thought, design, purpose, or intention. In context (2 Corinthians 2:5–11), Paul urges the church to forgive, comfort, and reaffirm love for a disciplined person so that excessive sorrow does not overwhelm them. His warning concerns Satan exploiting a failure to restore someone appropriately.

Deception is not always openly evil. Bitterness, division, and unforgiveness can deepen harm. The church must uphold truth without withholding grace when restoration is appropriate. Discernment includes examining our own responses, not only others’ behavior.

Understanding How Deception Operates

Scripture shows deception through distorted words, misleading appearances, and false spiritual claims.

Genesis 3:1–6 — The Distortion of God’s Word

The serpent begins with “Did God really say?” misrepresenting God’s command, then contradicts His warning and presents disobedience as a path to wisdom.

Eve sees the fruit as desirable (Genesis 3:6). What appears beneficial may still lead away from obedience when God’s Word is distorted.

2 Corinthians 11:13–15 — Deceptive Appearances

Paul warns that false apostles can disguise themselves as servants of Christ, just as Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. Religious language or impressive appearances are not proof of truth; teaching must be tested against the gospel.

1 John 4:1 — Testing the Spirits

John instructs believers to “test the spirits” rather than accept every claim (1 John 4:1). The surrounding verses emphasize confession about Jesus Christ. Testing is rooted in Scripture and the truth about Him, not constant suspicion.

Together, these passages teach us to test spiritual claims without treating every disagreement, difficult person, or uncomfortable feeling as demonic. Discernment should establish us in truth, not make us suspicious of everyone.

Question: How can we test something that sounds spiritual without becoming suspicious of everyone?

3 | Discernment is trained maturity (Hebrews 5:11–14)

Historical context: Hebrews addresses believers who have grown sluggish in learning. Milk and solid food illustrate the need to mature in understanding and practice, not differences in human worth.

Hebrews 5:11–12 — Growing Beyond the Basics

11 We have much to say about this, but it is hard to make it clear to you because you no longer try to understand. 12 In fact, though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you the elementary truths of God’s word all over again. You need milk, not solid food!

Verses 11–12 — Time spent hearing Scripture does not automatically produce maturity. The audience should be ready to teach but still needs the basics. Growth requires attentive learning and obedience, not listening alone (James 1:22).

Hebrews 5:13 — Understanding Righteousness

13 Anyone who lives on milk, being still an infant, is not acquainted with the teaching about righteousness.

Verse 13 — Those dependent on milk remain inexperienced in teaching about righteousness. Growing believers learn not only to recognize wrong in others but to understand and apply what is right before God in their own choices and relationships.

Hebrews 5:14 — Training Our Spiritual Senses

14 But solid food is for the mature, who by constant use have trained themselves to distinguish good from evil.

Verse 14 — The mature distinguish good from evil through “constant use.” The Greek gymnazō (γυμνάζω) means to train or exercise through practice; diakrisis (διάκρισις) means distinguishing or discernment. Discernment is developed through repeated engagement with and application of God’s Word, not intuition alone.

Feelings may reflect fear, experience, or incomplete understanding. We therefore seek God’s wisdom (James 1:5), wise counsel (Proverbs 11:14), and patience in listening (James 1:19).

Maturity also means humility: sometimes we recognize deception; other times we recognize that we lack enough understanding to judge. Knowing when to speak, remain silent, correct, or extend grace is part of submitting our responses to God.

Trained discernment prepares us to exercise Christ-given authority responsibly; Hebrews 5:14 describes practiced moral judgment, not merely an ability to identify demons.

4 | Authority belongs to Christ (Luke 10:17–20)

Context: Jesus sends disciples ahead of Him to announce the kingdom and minister in His name (Luke 10:1–16).

17 The seventy-two returned with joy and said, “Lord, even the demons submit to us in your name.” 18 He replied, “I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven. 19 I have given you authority to trample on snakes and scorpions and to overcome all the power of the enemy; nothing will harm you. 20 However, do not rejoice that the spirits submit to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven.”

The disciples’ authority was given for Jesus’ mission, but He directs their joy beyond spiritual power to the greater blessing of belonging to God.

Luke 10:17 — Authority in Jesus’ Name

Verse 17 — The disciples acknowledge that demons submit “in your name.” Their authority came from Jesus, not their own strength or maturity.

Luke 10:18–19 — Jesus’ Authority Over the Enemy

Verses 18–19 — Jesus’ words about Satan falling emphasize the defeat of enemy power as God’s kingdom advances. Snakes and scorpions picture danger and opposition; they are not a universal promise of freedom from physical harm.

Paul’s experience confirms this distinction: he commanded a spirit in Jesus’ name yet was later beaten and imprisoned. Authority does not mean control over every circumstance.

Luke 10:20 — Our Greatest Reason for Rejoicing

Verse 20 — Jesus tells the disciples to rejoice that their names are written in heaven. Belonging to God matters more than dramatic spiritual experiences.

Our identity rests in Christ, not in what we can accomplish or overcome. Spiritual authority must produce dependence and humility, not pride.

Our greatest victory is not that the enemy recognizes our authority, but that we belong to Jesus Christ.

Authority Requires Submission

James 4:7 supplies the order: 7 Submit yourselves, then, to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

The order matters: submit to God, then resist the devil. Resistance cannot be separated from dependence on Him.

Matthew 28:18 18 Then Jesus came to them and said, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.

Jesus declares that all authority in heaven and on earth belongs to Him (Matthew 28:18). We seek faithfulness to Christ, not power for ourselves. Paul’s later suffering reminds us that exercising authority does not remove every hardship.

Question: Why does Jesus redirect the disciples’ attention from their authority over evil spirits to their relationship with God, and what does this teach us about where our confidence should come from?

5 | Victory through faithfulness (Acts 16:19–34)

Verses 19–24: 19 When her owners realized that their hope of making money was gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace to face the authorities. 20 They brought them before the magistrates and said, “These men are Jews, and are throwing our city into an uproar 21 by advocating customs unlawful for us Romans to accept or practice.”

22 The crowd joined in the attack against Paul and Silas, and the magistrates ordered them to be stripped and beaten with rods. 23 After they had been severely flogged, they were thrown into prison, and the jailer was commanded to guard them carefully. 24 When he received these orders, he put them in the inner cell and fastened their feet in the stocks.

Verses 19–24 — The girl’s owners, angry over lost income, accuse Paul and Silas. The crowd and magistrates participate in their beating and imprisonment. Their suffering follows obedience, challenging the assumption that God’s direction always produces comfort.

Their imprisonment did not prove they were outside God’s will, nor must every hardship be labeled a direct satanic attack. The owners and authorities were accountable for their injustice. Ephesians 6:12 reminds us that our ultimate struggle is not against flesh and blood; people are not to be treated as the enemy.

Acts 16:25 — Worship in the Middle of Suffering

Verse 25: 25 About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the other prisoners were listening to them.

Their circumstances had not changed: they were still imprisoned and injured.

Verse 25 — Paul and Silas prayed and sang while other prisoners listened. Worship did not erase their pain; it showed continued dependence on God and became a witness in hardship.

Acts 16:26–28 — When God Opens the Prison Doors

Verses 26–28: 26 Suddenly there was such a violent earthquake that the foundations of the prison were shaken. At once all the prison doors flew open, and everyone’s chains came loose. 27 The jailer woke up, and when he saw the prison doors open, he drew his sword and was about to kill himself because he thought the prisoners had escaped. 28 But Paul shouted, “Don’t harm yourself! We are all here!”

Verses 26–28 — An earthquake opens the doors and loosens the chains. When the jailer fears the prisoners have escaped and prepares to harm himself, Paul calls out that they are still there. Instead of seeking revenge or ignoring him, Paul preserves his life. Victory can include mercy toward someone associated with our suffering.

Acts 16:29–34 — The Greater Purpose

Verses 29–34: 29 The jailer called for lights, rushed in and fell trembling before Paul and Silas. 30 He then brought them out and asked, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

31 They replied, “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved—you and your household.” 32 Then they spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all the others in his house. 33 At that hour of the night the jailer took them and washed their wounds; then immediately he and all his household were baptized. 34 The jailer brought them into his house and set a meal before them; he was filled with joy because he had come to believe in God—he and his whole household.

Verses 29–34 — The jailer asks how to be saved. Paul and Silas tell him to believe in Jesus and share the Word with his household. Afterward, the jailer washes their wounds, welcomes them into his home, and rejoices with his household after they believe and are baptized. The story’s climax is not merely opened doors but the gospel reaching new lives. Faithfulness and compassion become a witness even in suffering.

Connecting Back to Ephesians 6:11–13

Ephesians 6:11–13 calls believers to stand against the devil’s schemes while remembering that people are not ultimately the enemy. In Acts 16, Paul and Silas stand firm through injustice by praying, worshiping, showing mercy, and proclaiming Christ. Standing firm does not always mean escaping hardship; sometimes it means remaining faithful within it.

Question: What does this account teach about victory when obedience does not immediately remove suffering?

6 | Connect the entire Armor of God series

Putting on the armor of God means testing everything by Scripture, choosing integrity even when it is hard, and keeping the gospel and reconciliation at the center. It means trusting God when the doors have not opened yet, finding our identity in belonging to Christ, knowing how to handle God’s Word rightly, and staying prayerful and dependent on Him in every situation.
